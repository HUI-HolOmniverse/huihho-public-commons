## The Factory Loop

Ocean Star is not merely a floating city — it is a **factory of Ocean Star constellations**. It reproduces and expands its own buoyancy by recycling the waste material of the different vessels that park there.

- Vessels park and offload composite and metal waste.
- The site converts that waste into high-performance materials on the spot — WPC is the current working baseline, not the fixed formula.
- Each new shell becomes both flotation and feedstock for the next ring outward.
- The parked fleet is not cargo — it is the **quarry**.
- Buoyancy grows by eating what arrives: more traffic means more feedstock means more expansion means more traffic. Expansion is the byproduct of operations, not a separate construction phase.

This is R-83's self-replicating, self-reinforcing mechanism made operational. Every unit of reserve is potential structure; every unit of waste is potential reserve.

## Vessel-Return Contracts

Contracts may be made whereby, when Ocean Star receives a certain amount of input ingredients from a given vessel, a certain percentage — however small — of the recycled output can go back to that vessel itself when it prefers, instead of being exchanged for discounts on services to the ship.

- The return is **material**, not a discount: the vessel receives shells, composite stock, or other recycled product it can use or trade elsewhere.
- The percentage is negotiable per contract and scales with input volume.
- This turns the waste-for-service exchange into a **two-way material economy**: vessels supply feedstock, receive product, and the site's expansion is funded by the difference.
- Service discounts remain available as an alternative settlement path, but material return is the preferred and default clause where contracts allow.

This baseline is locked for the moment but explicitly open to any equivalent or superior formula that proves better.
